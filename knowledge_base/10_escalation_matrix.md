# Escalation Matrix

Document ID: SOP-010 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Operations Head
Note: synthetic prototype document for MacroOps AI. Values are prototype assumptions.

## Who is the first contact for each problem?
- Slow picking, queue in the store, missing items on the floor: store supervisor.
- Rider not assigned or late pickup: store dispatcher, then the dispatch lead.
- Rider behaviour or performance: rider coordinator.
- Stockouts, phantom stock, replenishment: inventory lead.
- Late delivery complaints, refunds, compensation: customer support lead.
- Staffing gaps and store level SLA problems: store manager, then area manager.

## What does the store manager handle?
The store manager handles anything inside one store: queues, short staffing for a shift, repeated missing items and Level 1 SLA alerts. The store manager must act within 15 minutes of an alert and call the area manager if the problem is not fixed in 30 minutes.

## What does the dispatch lead handle?
The dispatch lead handles rider supply across stores. This covers rider shortages, moving riders between stores, incentives and rain plans. Response time is 10 minutes.

## What does the area manager handle?
The area manager handles Level 2 alerts, staffing support across stores, incident declarations and approved promised time extensions. The area manager responds within 30 minutes and files a corrective action plan within 24 hours for a Level 2 alert.

## What does the operations head handle?
The operations head handles Level 3 network alerts, system outages longer than 30 minutes and decisions that affect the whole network, such as pausing promotions or changing the SLA target.

## What are the response time targets?
Store supervisor: 5 minutes. Store dispatcher: 5 minutes for HIGH risk alerts. Store manager: 15 minutes. Dispatch lead: 10 minutes. Area manager: 30 minutes. Operations head: 1 hour.

## When should an issue be escalated to the next level?
Escalate when the problem is not fixed within the response time of the current level, when more than one store in an area is affected, or when customers are being compensated for delays at scale.
