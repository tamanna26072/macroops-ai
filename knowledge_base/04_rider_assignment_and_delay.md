# Rider Assignment and Delay Handling

Document ID: SOP-004 | Version 1.0 | Owner: Dispatch Lead
Note: synthetic prototype document for MacroOps AI. Values are prototype assumptions.

## Assignment delay
Assignment delay is the time between the order being packed and a rider being assigned. The target is under 3 minutes on average. Long assignment delay is the strongest predictor of an SLA breach, because the packed order waits while the clock keeps running.

## Dispatcher actions by delay
- Over 5 minutes: the dispatcher widens the rider search radius to 3 km and re-sends the order.
- Over 10 minutes: the dispatcher assigns the nearest available rider manually and may offer a small incentive.
- Over 15 minutes: the dispatcher informs the store manager and considers extending the promised time for the customer.

## Rider shortage
When more than 20 percent of orders in a store wait for a rider, the dispatch lead moves riders from low-volume stores nearby. During rain, rider speed drops by about a third, so the dispatch lead adds extra riders and extends promised times by 5 minutes.

## Long distance orders
Orders over 6 km should be assigned to riders with the best on-time record. Riders whose average speed is under 11 km per hour or whose breach rate is above 40 percent are reviewed by the rider coordinator.

## Rider performance review
The rider coordinator reviews riders with at least 30 orders in a month. Riders with a breach rate above 40 percent get coaching on route choice and handover time. Repeated poor performance is escalated to the area manager.
